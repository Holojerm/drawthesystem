# Interviewer notes (hidden)
Crux: double-entry ledger entries (every transaction writes balanced debit/credit rows across accounts, never mutates a running "balance" column in place) is what makes both atomicity and point-in-time reconstruction fall out for free; a client-supplied idempotency key stored with a unique constraint is what makes a retried request a no-op instead of a second transaction.
Probes: what "atomic across multiple account updates" means concretely (a single DB transaction, or a saga with compensation, and why one over the other at this scale); how current balance is derived — summed from history on read, or a maintained running total that's still reconciled against the ledger periodically; what a corrected/reversed transaction looks like (a new offsetting entry, never editing or deleting history).
Red flags: a mutable `balance` column updated in place with no immutable history backing it (reconciliation and point-in-time queries become impossible); "we'll just retry and hope it's fine" for idempotency; deleting or editing a bad transaction instead of reversing it with a new entry.
